# Phase 5: flight model calibration against PX4 SITL

The sim's flight model (spec 2.7) is fitted to the Phase 4 SITL flights. SITL has no power
model, so every energy figure here is the Phase 4 proxy (spec A57). Every fitted value stays a
placeholder, flagged `assumption = true`, with source `px4-sitl-proxy`.

Everything is reproduced by `pipeline/run.sh calib`, from the logs in `px4/runs/` (git-ignored,
regenerated by `px4/tools/run_batch.sh accept`).

## 1. Audit (before fitting)

`pipeline/calib/audit.py` writes `pipeline/calib/out/audit.json`.

**PX4 build.** The SITL build is PX4 v1.17.0, commit `d6f12ad1c4f70ad3230afd7d86e971421e02fef4`.
`~/PX4-Autopilot` carries quad-autonomy-sim's two patches:

- `0001-uxrce-dds-export-estimation-topics` adds extra uXRCE-DDS topics.
- `0002-gz-bridge-use-wmm-mag-sim` makes gz_bridge defer to PX4's magnetometer simulator.

Neither affects thrust, mass or the flight controller.

**The vehicle against the scenario.**

| Payload (kg) | Gazebo airframe + latch + pod (kg) | Max thrust (N) | Thrust margin (max thrust / weight) | Hover throttle with pod | Hover thrust / weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 9.0 + 0.6 + 0.001 = 9.601 | 313.8 | 3.33 | 0.540 | 0.981 |
| 2 | 9.0 + 0.6 + 2.0 = 11.6 | 313.8 | 2.76 | 0.603 | 1.004 |
| 5 | 9.0 + 0.6 + 5.0 = 14.6 | 313.8 | 2.19 | 0.676 | 1.002 |
| 10 | 9.0 + 0.6 + 10.0 = 19.6 | 313.8 | 1.63 | 0.783 | 1.002 |

- The airframe is PX4's x500 body brought to the scenario's `drone.empty_mass_kg` of 9.0 kg, with
  the scenario's 0.6 kg latch (spec A55). Vehicle and sim config therefore have the same mass by
  construction.
- The motor constant is 7.845e-5 N/(rad/s)², which gives thrust-to-weight 2.0 at the 16 kg MTOW.
- The throttle is the commanded rotor speed over 1000 rad/s. Empty hover after the release is
  0.542 at every payload.
- Hover thrust from the commanded speeds matches the weight within 2%, so the motor model and
  the masses agree.

**Proxy constants.**

| Constant | Value |
|---|---|
| Rotors | 4 |
| Rotor disc | 0.06122 m² (x500 propeller, radius 0.1396 m) |
| Air density | 1.225 kg/m³ |
| Figure of merit | 1 (ideal induced power, no profile power) |
| Motor and ESC efficiency | 1 |

So the proxy's absolute level is a lower bound on what a real vehicle would draw; only ratios
carry over.

**Why cruise power rises 3.0x from 0 to 10 kg.** Pure mass^1.5 gives (19.6 / 9.601)^1.5 = 2.92.
The measured 2.998 corresponds to an effective empty mass of 9.27 kg, against the real 9.601 kg.

It is not a bug and not a constant term in the proxy. In the lane at 15 m/s the rotors carry
only 0.951 of the weight at 0 kg and 0.968 at 10 kg: Gazebo's rotor-drag force supplies the
rest, and it is a smaller share of a heavier vehicle. Uneven rotor thrust adds a little, because
induced power is convex in thrust: the ratio grows from 1.0024 to 1.0059.

Together these give (0.9682 / 0.9509)^1.5 x (1.0059 / 1.0024) = 1.031, and 2.917 x 1.031 = 3.007,
which accounts for the 2.998. The vehicle is the scaled x500 it was meant to be, and the proxy
is unchanged.

## 2. Per-phase data

`pipeline/calib/phases.py` cuts every fit log into four phases at the drone's spec transitions.
It writes one row per run and phase to `pipeline/calib/out/phases.csv`: 15 runs, 60 rows. That
includes every seed and the 2x run.

The phases are:

- Takeoff: D01 to D02, ground to cruise height.
- Cruise: the lane, D02 to D03.
- Approach + landing: D04 to D05. This is a 61 m descent over the granted spot, the marker
  lock, then precision land to disarm.
- Hop: IN to OUT, flown empty because the pod was released on IN.

Distances come from ground truth; power and energy from the proxy.

Means per payload (5 kg is the mean of 12 runs):

| Phase | Payload (kg) | Mass (kg) | Duration (s) | Horizontal flown (m) | Vertical flown (m) | Mean power (W) | Energy (kJ) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| takeoff | 0 / 2 / 5 / 10 | 9.6 / 11.6 / 14.6 / 19.6 | 25.9 / 26.4 / 26.8 / 27.3 | 1-2 | 60.7 | 1023 / 1349 / 1896 / 2921 | 26.4 / 35.6 / 50.8 / 79.7 |
| cruise | 0 / 2 / 5 / 10 | 9.6 / 11.6 / 14.6 / 19.6 | 148.4 / 146.8 / 146.5 / 146.5 | 2094.2 / 2092.7 / 2092.5 / 2092.5 | 1.8 | 1107 / 1479 / 2110 / 3318 | 164.3 / 217.1 / 309.2 / 486.0 |
| approach + landing | 0 / 2 / 5 / 10 | 9.6 / 11.6 / 14.6 / 19.6 | 46.6 / 49.0 / 49.0 / 49.0 | 4-7 | 60.8 | 1104 / 1471 / 2076 / 3225 | 51.5 / 72.1 / 101.7 / 158.0 |
| hop | all | 9.6 | 22.5-23.4 | 7.0 | 4.4 | 868-881 | 19.5-20.6 |

**Along-track distance against lane length.** Every cruise flew 2092.4 to 2094.2 m of
horizontal track for the 2093.4 m lane, so no flight deviates from the lane by more than 0.1%.

- The drone starts on A's OUT spot, 2 m east of A's centre (plus its seeded offset), and the lane
  ends over B's centre, so the straight line it covers is about 2092.4 m.
- The 0 kg run flew 1.8 m more, settling at the fix.
- The mean speed over the whole lane is 14.28 m/s. The plateau is 15.03 m/s, and the rest is the
  speed-up and slow-down at 2 m/s².

The takeoff and hop means include time on the ground: priming offboard before arming, and the
2 s PX4 waits after touchdown before it disarms. Those seconds are part of what the sim's
`drone.takeoff_s` and `drone.hop_s` stand for, so they stay in.

## 3. The placeholder model against the logs

`sim flight-model --lane-m L --payload-kg P [--config <scenario.toml>]` prints the core's own
time and energy for a visit's flight phases (`VisitEstimate` in `sim/src/flight.rs`):

- Takeoff and approach take `drone.takeoff_s` and `drone.approach_s` at hover power with the pod.
- The lane is flown by the flight model.
- The hop takes `drone.hop_s` at hover power, empty.

The calibration scripts call it, so nothing in Python reimplements the model.

`pipeline/calib/residuals.py` sets the placeholder profile, the Pittsburgh scenario as of
Phase 4, against the measured means. The residual is (measured - predicted) / measured; the full
table is in `out/residuals_placeholder.json`.

| Payload (kg) | Visit time predicted / measured (s) | Time residual | Visit energy predicted / measured (kJ) | Energy residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 234.6 / 243.8 | +3.8% | 135.2 / 262.1 | +48.4% |
| 2 | 234.6 / 245.2 | +4.3% | 175.4 / 344.9 | +49.1% |
| 5 | 234.6 / 244.8 | +4.2% | 242.4 / 481.2 | +49.6% |
| 10 | 234.6 / 246.2 | +4.7% | 369.9 / 744.4 | +50.3% |

**Time.** The placeholder's visit time is already within 5%, but only because its errors cancel:

| Phase | Placeholder | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| Takeoff | 30 s | 26-27 s |
| Hop | 20 s | 22.5-23.4 s |
| Approach | 45 s | 49 s |
| Lane | 139.6 s (L / 15 m/s) | 146.5 s (with the speed-up and slow-down) |

**Energy.** The proxy is about twice the placeholder in every phase. The placeholder's 1000 W
cruise and 1200 W hover at 14.6 kg compare with a measured 2110 W and about 2080 W. The ratio is
nearly the same at every payload: its mass^1.5 shape is right and its scale is not.

## 4. The fit

`pipeline/run.sh calib` runs audit, phases, residuals and fit, in that order. The fit
(`pipeline/calib/fit.py`) uses the 15 Phase 4 runs: payload 0, 2, 5 and 10 kg, ten seeds at
5 kg, and the 2x run. It writes `pipeline/calib/fitted_params.json` and `pipeline/calib/out/fit.json`.

The fit is deterministic: the bootstrap seed is fixed and the inputs are sorted. Two
consecutive runs gave byte-identical outputs (sha256).

**Model.** It is the core's own (spec 2.7, `VisitEstimate`):

```
visit time   = takeoff_s + L / cruise_speed_mps + approach_s + hop_s
cruise power = cruise_power_ref_w * (m / power_ref_mass_kg)^1.5     the lane
hover power  = hover_power_ref_w  * (m / power_ref_mass_kg)^1.5     takeoff, approach, hop (empty)
```

**Parameters.** Uncertainty is the standard deviation over a 2000-draw bootstrap over runs. All
values are flagged `assumption = true` with source px4-sitl-proxy.

| Parameter | Placeholder | Fitted | Bootstrap std | How |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cruise_speed_mps` | 15.0 | 15.033 | < 0.001 | median ground speed over the middle half of the lane |
| `takeoff_s` | 30 | 34.18 | 0.07 | takeoff (26.7 s) plus the lane's speed-up and slow-down (7.44 s) |
| `approach_s` | 45 | 48.85 | 0.16 | D04 to D05 |
| `hop_s` | 20 | 22.63 | 0.15 | IN to OUT |
| `cruise_power_ref_w` | 1000 | 2107.9 | 3.2 | least squares, cruise class |
| `hover_power_ref_w` | 1200 | 1830.5 | 2.0 | least squares, hover class |
| `power_ref_mass_kg` | 14.6 | 14.6 | - | kept |

**Choices the data forced.**

- *Speed and lane overhead.* One lane length cannot separate a cruise speed from a constant
  overhead, so the speed is measured directly. The overhead is put into `takeoff_s` because the
  core times a lane as exactly L / v. Its energy is then charged at hover power, which is within
  13% of cruise power.
- *Payload dependence of times.* The regression slope of each duration on payload:

  | Phase | Slope (s/kg) | p | Effect from 0 to 10 kg |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Takeoff | 0.144 | < 0.001 | 1.4 s |
  | Lane | -0.174 | 0.003 | -1.7 s; the 0 kg run settled longer at the fix |
  | Approach | 0.198 | 0.009 | 2.0 s |
  | Hop | 0.024 | 0.77 | 0.2 s |

  Three are significant at 5%. But 0, 2 and 10 kg have one run each and the 5 kg repeats barely
  vary, so small effects test significant. Each is under 1% of a 245 s visit and below the 5%
  time target, and the spec has no payload-dependent timing key. The times stay constant (spec
  A62).
- *Residuals.* The cruise class fits within 1.6% at every payload. The hover class misses by up
  to 14% per phase: approach +12% (the 61 m descent and precision land run at near-cruise power),
  hop -12% (ground idle before and after it), takeoff +3%.

  These residuals vary by phase, not by mass or speed, so a drag term cannot fix them, and none
  is added. The spec has one hover power per drone; a per-phase factor would need a spec change
  and is not made (A63). At visit level the phases offset: see the cross-validation below.

**Leave-one-payload-out cross-validation.** Fit without one payload, then predict its visits
with the core's model.

| Held-out payload | Time error | Energy error |
|---|---|---|
| 0 kg | +0.5% | -0.9% |
| 2 kg | -0.1% | -2.1% |
| 5 kg (12 runs) | -0.2% to +0.5% | -2.8% to -3.2% |
| 10 kg | -0.6% | -4.0% |

Worst cases: time 0.6% and energy 4.0%, against the 5% and 15% targets. Energy is predicted
low, because the approach's under-prediction outweighs the hop's over-prediction.
